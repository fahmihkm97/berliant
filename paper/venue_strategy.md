# Venue Strategy

Status date: 2026-08-11

## Primary Target — ICST 2027

IEEE International Conference on Software Testing,
Verification and Validation.

Research-paper deadline:

2026-11-02 AoE

Why it fits:

- software testing is the central research problem;
- stochastic fault localization is directly relevant;
- empirical research is explicitly in scope;
- BSIB provides a reproducible testing benchmark;
- SCIF is primarily a testing/discovery method rather than
  an AI-model algorithm.

Recommended positioning:

Residual-risk-guided stochastic interaction discovery for
capability-rich software and AI systems.

## Stretch Target — FSE 2027

Research-paper deadline:

2026-10-02

Why it fits:

- software testing;
- debugging and fault localization;
- dependability and reliability;
- software engineering for AI.

Main risk:

The current evidence is synthetic and the method has not yet been
validated on a real production system.

A real-world validation experiment would materially strengthen
an FSE submission.

## AI-Engineering Alternative — CAIN 2027

Research-track deadline:

2026-10-30

Why it fits:

- software engineering for AI-enabled systems;
- agentic software;
- quality assurance;
- reliability;
- next-generation AI-enabled systems.

Main risk:

The current SCIF method is generic enough to apply beyond AI, while
the experimental evidence is synthetic.

For CAIN, the manuscript should include a stronger concrete
AI-enabled-system validation.

## Future Testing Target — ISSTA 2027

ISSTA is directly aligned with software testing and analysis.

The 2027 edition is scheduled for Singapore.

At the time of this venue audit, the official ISSTA series page lists
the 2027 edition but does not yet provide its submission deadline.

ISSTA should remain a candidate if additional validation is completed
before its call for papers becomes available.

## Current Recommendation

Prepare the manuscript to ICST 2027 standards first.

Before submission, prioritize:

1. real-system validation if feasible;
2. artifact reproducibility;
3. novelty comparison with MFS/FIC/FDA-CIT/FROG/NoPend;
4. confidence-interval reporting;
5. clear synthetic-evaluation limitations;
6. anonymized artifact preparation.
